Slide 18

Discussion: General Perceptions

Averages were high across the board:

60.4% always or often felt a sense of belonging.

76.8% felt very safe.

55.3% felt that the libraries are welcoming.

76.6 felt the libraries are very respectful of diversity

A lower percentage of Black/African-American respondents felt the libraries are very respectful of diversity.

A lower percentage of White library users felt that libraries were very welcoming.

Slide 19

How does gender affect perceptions?

Males have a slightly higher sense of belonging than females, with the largest gender gap among Black/African-American respondents.

Males feel significantly safer than females, indicating a notable gender disparity in perceived safety, particularly among White respondents.

Males feel more welcomed than females, with the largest gap seen among Black/African-American respondents.

Females have a more positive view of the library's respect for diversity compared to males, with a significant gap between male and female Black/African-American respondents.

Slide 28

“Inclusion for All” Framework

When implementing diversity initiatives, it is important to minimize backlash from dominant groups, which negatively impacts people from intentionally marginalized backgrounds.

Goal: To achieve inclusion without triggering reactance from dominant groups.

Source: Brannon, T. N., Carter, E. R., Murdock‐Perriera, L. A., & Higginbotham, G. D. (2018). From backlash to inclusion for all: Instituting diversity efforts to maximize benefits across group lines. Social issues and policy review, 12(1), 57-90. https://doi.org/10.1111/sipr.12040

Slide 29

Evidence of backlash to diversity Initiatives?

Some evidence of lower engagement or satisfaction among white respondents on two key items:

Whether the library has a welcoming atmosphere

Finding materials by creators from diverse backgrounds

Some evidence of lower engagement or satisfaction among Black/African-American respondents on the diversity of materials in the collection

No strong evidence of widespread backlash, but these numbers are worth keeping an eye on in the future.

Slide 30

Sources of backlash and how to address them:

Perceived Restriction of Independence

Solution: Provide choice and autonomy

Preference for the Status Quo and Colorblindness

Solution: Structured environments for intergroup interactions

Beliefs that Racial and Other Social Equalities Have Been Reached

Solution: Teach background on racial inequality. Emphasize fairness and legitimacy of diversity efforts

Methodology

Type of Research:

Quantitative Analysis of data from the U-M Library Campus-Wide Survey.

Data delimitation:

“What is your most primary role at U-M?”

Selected one of the following: “Faculty member, Graduate student, Postdoctoral fellow, Visiting researcher or scholar, House officer.”

During the past two years, have you been the leader of an original research or creative project? (As a principal investigator, a project director, a lead author, a dissertation writer, a project grant awardee, etc.)

Selected “Yes”.

“What broad category (or categories) best describe your research and academic work?”

Selected “Medical and Health Sciences”.

“In your research and creative work, do you engage in any of the following activities?”

Selected “Seeking funding”.

Slide 57

RQ 2. What issues do these researchers encounter in accessing and utilizing library resources?

Qualitative Responses:

Awareness of services

Comment 1: "I feel like it is hard to find information on how I can analyze my data using the library's resources. I don't even know if the information exists. I usually go to CSCAR for analysis of my data. The campus feels very disconnected and I don’t know how the library can tie into any of this."

Comment 2: "I signed up to get alerts of funding and none of the suggested funding sources apply to me. I was unaware that the library could assist with the other research-related tasks and want to know more about specifically how they can help with these."

Accessing library services is not convenient

Comment 1: "The most helpful was meeting with a librarian. The websites are too confusing to navigate sometimes."

Comment 2: "Since physical browsing of engineering books has been removed, could you please have an online browsing option where the book offerings are sorted by topic with titles listed? The search tools are really really bad."

Slide 58

RQ 3. What improvements can be made to library services to better support interdisciplinary research in these fields?

Summary

Overall, there’s a similar trends from different numbers of interdisciplinary research respondents.

Respondents are actively engaged in a variety of information-seeking activities, primarily reviewing literature, managing sources, gathering data, seeking funding, and sharing data.

The library's support in research is generally seen as adequate and satisfied.

General search engines, U-M library's online catalog, and scholarly databases are the most commonly used sources for background material.

Improving awareness, ease to access, and demonstrating the relevance and usefulness of library services can increase the utilization.
